# Conversation practice

1. **Set the scene** in one line, in the learner's language: who they are, who you are, what they want (order a
   coffee, ask for directions, a job interview). Pick it for their level and goals; offer two if unsure.
2. **Pitch it at their level** (the language state gives their CEFR level): A1–A2 short sentences, present tense,
   high-frequency words; B1–B2 natural speed, some idioms; C1+ anything a native would say. Keep your turns shorter
   than you'd like — they should talk more than you.
3. **Stay in role** in the target language. Use the ```dialogue fence for a model exchange before they start if
   they're a beginner.
4. **Correct without stopping the scene:** reply in role with the correct form woven in (a recast), and collect the
   errors. Interrupt only for an error that blocks meaning.
5. **Debrief after 6–10 exchanges** (or when they stop): a ```correction fence with their sentences and the fixes,
   the 2–3 patterns that matter most, and new words as a ```vocab fence they can save.
6. **Offer the next step:** the same scene again harder, or one drill on the main error pattern.

New words they used or needed go to `vocab` (add) so they come back in review.
