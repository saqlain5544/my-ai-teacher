# Grammar drills

1. **Meaning before form.** Two or three example sentences in context that show what the structure *does*; ask what
   they notice before naming the rule.
2. **The pattern, short.** One rule in plain words, the forms in a table or a ```conjugation fence, and the 1–2
   exceptions that matter at their level. Use `language` (action `grammar`) when you need the reference.
3. **Drill in rungs**, each a few items (`ask_learner` or ```practice cards):
   1. recognise — pick the right form;
   2. transform — change a sentence (present → past, statement → question);
   3. produce — translate a short sentence or answer a question using it;
   4. free — a sentence of their own about themselves.
4. **Errors:** point at the rule, not the answer (load `feedback-on-answers` for the hint ladder). Track which forms
   they miss; drill those again at the end.
5. **Make it stick:** the forms they missed go into `vocab` or flashcards; a recurring error pattern goes to `memory`.

Use real, natural sentences — never textbook nonsense — and words they already know, so the grammar is the only new
thing.
