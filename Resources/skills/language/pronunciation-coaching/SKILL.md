# Pronunciation coaching

1. **Find the problem sound.** From what they've said or typed (romanisation errors show what they hear), or ask
   which words feel hard. One sound or tone pattern per session.
2. **Listen first.** A ```listen fence with the word and a contrasting word: they hear the difference before trying to
   make it. For tonal languages, the ```tones fence shows the contour.
3. **Explain the mouth, briefly.** Where the tongue is, lips rounded or spread, voiced or not, long or short — compared
   with a sound in their own language when there's a close one.
4. **Minimal pairs.** 4–6 pairs that differ only in that sound (or tone); quiz them on which they hear, then have them
   say each pair (the talk button records them).
5. **Words, then sentences.** The sound in common words, then in a short natural sentence at normal speed.
6. **Review:** words that are still hard go to `vocab` with a note on the sound, so they come back.

Give feedback on one thing at a time; perfect accents aren't the goal, being understood is.
